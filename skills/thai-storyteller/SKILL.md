# Thai Storyteller — Pocket Book & Short Story Writing

Write Thai pocket books and short stories with natural, dialogue-rich prose. No translation artifacts. No AI-isms.

## PRIME DIRECTIVES — Read Before Any Writing

**DIRECTIVE 0: WRITE IN NATURAL THAI.**
No English sentence structures wearing Thai words. If it sounds like a translation, rewrite. Read every dialogue line aloud — if you stumble, the reader will too.

**DIRECTIVE 1: OUTLINE BEFORE PROSE.**
Never write blind. Characters, chapter breakdown, premise — all on paper before the first word of narrative. Get Papa's approval on the outline.

**DIRECTIVE 2: ONE CHAPTER, ONE SAVE.**
Write a chapter. Save it immediately. Confirm. Then move to the next. Never batch chapters in memory — if the session dies, the work survives.

**DIRECTIVE 3: CHECK GENRE CONVENTIONS.**
Before choosing tone, register, or pacing — consult the genre table. A romance scene in horror register breaks immersion instantly.

## PRE-FLIGHT CHECKLIST

Before writing a single line:

1. **Genre?** รัก / ดราม่า / สยองขวัญ / แฟนตาซี / ตลก / วัยรุ่น
2. **Length?** Number of chapters, target words per chapter (default: 2000)
3. **POV?** บุรุษที่ 1 (I/ผม/หนู) or บุรุษที่ 3 (he/she/เขา/เธอ)
4. **Register?** ทางการ / กึ่งทางการ / กันเอง / ภาษาวัยรุ่น
5. **Setting?** Time period, location, cultural context
6. **Characters loaded?** At least protagonist + antagonist named and described

## CRITICAL LAWs

**LAW 1: DIALOGUE MUST SOUND SPOKEN, NOT WRITTEN.**
Read every line aloud. If it sounds like a textbook or a translated subtitle, rewrite. Thai speech uses fragments, interruptions, and filler words (เอ่อ, คือ, แบบว่า). Written Thai prose is NOT spoken Thai — dialogue is the latter.

**LAW 2: NEVER MIX WESTERN AND THAI NAMING CONVENTIONS.**
"สมชายคุยกับJenniferที่ซอยทาวน์เฮ้าส์" → instant credibility collapse. If the setting is Thai, names are Thai. If it's international, justify every non-Thai name with backstory. Hybrid names (ไทย-อังกฤษ) are fine ONLY for luk-kreung characters where it's plot-relevant.

**LAW 3: EVERY CHAPTER MUST END WITH TENSION.**
Cliffhanger, emotional punch, unanswered question, or foreshadowing. NO flat endings. The reader must want to turn the page. Even in romance — end on a look, a touch, a text message arriving. Make them need the next chapter.

**LAW 4: SHOW, DON'T TELL.**
Not "เขาโกรธ" → "กำปั้นของเขากำแน่น กรามขบจนเป็นสัน"  
Not "เธอเสียใจ" → "น้ำตาร่วงลงบนข้อความในโทรศัพท์ก่อนที่เธอจะทันได้อ่านซ้ำ"  
Not "อากาศร้อน" → "เหงื่อซึมตามไรผม ยางมะตอยส่งไอความร้อนระยิบระยับ"

## GENRE CONVENTIONS TABLE

| Genre | Register | Pacing | Chapter End | Common Tropes | Dialogue Style |
|-------|----------|--------|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| รักวัยรุ่น | กันเอง | Fast | Emotional beat | First love, school, LINE chats, friendzone | Short, playful, emoji-implied |
| ดราม่า | กึ่งทางการ | Medium | Moral dilemma | Family secrets, sacrifice, class divide | Weighted pauses, what's unsaid |
| สยองขวัญ | บรรยายเข้ม | Slow build | Dread escalation | Rural ghosts, folklore, isolation, guilt | Short sentences, sensory dread |
| แฟนตาซี | ผสม | Variable | Revelation | Thai mythology, reincarnation, magic cost | Archaic particles sparingly |
| ตลก | กันเองสุด | Fast | Punchline/Irony | Slapstick, wordplay, social satire | Snappy, exaggerated, timing-driven |
| สืบสวน | กึ่งทางการ | Medium-tight | Clue/Red herring | Crime, corruption, small-town secrets | Terse, information-dense |

## PITFALLS

### Dialogue
- **ครับ/ค่ะ every line:** Sounds robotic in casual scenes. Drop them for friends/family talking. Keep for strangers/elders/formality-required scenes.
- **Transliterated English idioms:** "Break a leg" → "ขาหัก" is nonsense. Find Thai equivalent (โชคดี) or rewrite the sentiment naturally.
- **All characters sound the same:** Age, region, education → different vocabulary, different sentence length. A grandma doesn't talk like a university student.

### Prose
- **Paragraphs over 5 lines:** Too dense for pocket book format (mobile reading). Break at natural pauses.
- **Passive voice overuse (ถูก...):** Thai passive carries a negative connotation (ถูกทำร้าย = was harmed). Don't use it for neutral descriptions.
- **POV shift mid-chapter:** One POV per scene minimum. Head-hopping confuses readers.
- **Over-description of visuals:** Thai readers value emotion and relationship dynamics over landscape descriptions. One vivid detail beats three paragraphs of scenery.

### Culture
- **Royal family references:** Require respectful register (ราชาศัพท์). If unsure, avoid direct references entirely.
- **Regional dialects:** อีสาน ≠ เหนือ ≠ ใต้. Pick one and use authentic vocabulary, not Central Thai with a few regional words sprinkled in.
- **Modern slang in period pieces:** "เฟียส" in a 1990s setting = anachronism. Check era-appropriate language.
- **Monk/religious references:** Handle with respect. Don't use monks as comic relief.

### Structure
- **No outline = meandering plot.** Always outline first (DIRECTIVE 1).
- **Flat chapter endings:** Violates LAW 3. Fix with: cliffhanger, emotional beat, new information, or foreshadowing.
- **Forgotten characters:** If you introduce someone in chapter 2, they need to matter by chapter 5 or get cut.

## SENTENCE-FINAL PARTICLES QUICK REFERENCE

| Particle | Vibe | Who Uses It | Example |
|----------|------|-------------|---------|
| ครับ/ค่ะ | Polite | Strangers, elders, formal scenes | "เข้าใจครับ" |
| จ๊ะ | Affectionate | Older to younger, close friends | "มากินข้าวกันจ๊ะ" |
| จ้ะ | Firm-affectionate | Parent to child, teacher | "อย่าลืมทำการบ้านจ้ะ" |
| ดิ | Casual-insistent | Friends, siblings | "ไปเถอะดิ" / "บอกมาดิ" |
| วะ | Rough-casual | Close male friends, angry moments | "มึงจะเอาอะไรวะ" |
| เหรอ/หรอ | Questioning | Universal casual | "จริงเหรอ?" / "ไปหรอ?" |
| อ่ะ | Soft-casual | Teens, close friends | "ไม่รู้ดิ" → "ไม่รู้อ่ะ" |
| ป่ะ | Invitation | Friends | "ไปกินข้าวกันป่ะ?" |
| สิ | Certainty | Universal | "ใช่สิ" / "ก็ดีสิ" |
| เถอะ | Persuasion/Let's | Universal | "ไปเถอะ" / "พอเถอะ" |
| ละ/แล้ว | Already/now | Universal | "ไปละนะ" / "หิวจังเลย" |

Pro tip: mix particles within a conversation. A character who uses "ครับ" for every sentence sounds like a robot. Real people shift: polite to stranger → casual with friends → rough when angry — all within the same chapter.
